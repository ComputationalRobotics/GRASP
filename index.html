Method overview

Evaluation details

A GRASP task succeeds when mean object position error is below 10 cm and mean rotation error is below 0.5 rad. Execution failures count as failures. Error means use jointly finite position–rotation error pairs. Baseline success rates follow their original reporting.

Abstract

Robots increasingly learn skills from retargeted human motion, and the physical quality of these references matters for policy learning. Physics-based retargeting produces physically grounded references by optimizing, in simulation, robot actions whose motion tracks a human demonstration. Retargeted motion is contact rich: hands form and release grasps, and humanoids make and break foot contacts. Tempered sequential Monte Carlo (TSMC) has shown promise for challenging trajectory optimization by combining population-based exploration with gradient-informed refinement, but it differentiates through the dynamics, and contact transitions make these gradients difficult to obtain or unreliable.

We introduce GRASP, an extension of TSMC that recovers search directions from evaluated rollouts, enabling contact-rich trajectory optimization without differentiating through the simulator. The key idea is to reuse trajectory–cost pairs, including rejected proposals, to fit local quadratic models whose gradients guide subsequent particle moves. Each rollout thus contributes both a candidate motion and information for improving the search. We characterize the recovered directions through a Gaussian-smoothed objective, connecting local regression to optimization across nonsmooth contact transitions. Experiments demonstrate effective trajectory optimization under smooth dynamics and discrete contact changes.

We evaluate GRASP on physics-based motion retargeting, where gradient-guided sampling in a receding-horizon loop realizes diverse contact-rich interactions under a standard tracking objective. The same formulation applies across embodiments, including bimanual dexterous hands of different kinematics and actuation as well as full-body humanoids, without per-embodiment redesign of the objective. It also scales to large datasets, retargeting thousands of human demonstrations from HOT3D and OakInk2. These results demonstrate that GRASP converts human demonstrations into physically feasible robot trajectories across embodiments and at scale.
